# Food Processing & Neutralization Methods

This guide documents every known processing method that can reduce or eliminate harmful compounds in foods, including the mechanism of action, effectiveness, and conditions required.

---

## Methods Overview

| Method | Best For | Key Limitations |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| Boiling | Oxalates, lectins, phytates, nitrates, HCN-precursors | Discard water; water-soluble nutrients also leach out |
| Blanching | Oxalates, enzyme deactivation | Less effective than full boiling |
| Soaking | Lectins, phytates, saponins, tannins | Must discard soak water |
| Pressure cooking | Lectins, phytates | Very effective for legumes |
| Roasting/Baking | Lectins, some aflatoxins, HCN in seeds | Does not eliminate heat-stable toxins |
| Fermentation | Phytates, lactose, some lectins, nitrates | Requires time and proper culture |
| Sprouting | Phytates, lectins, trypsin inhibitors | Takes 2–5 days |
| Peeling | Pesticide residues, glycoalkaloids | Only for surface contaminants |
| Acidification | Some antinutrients, pathogen reduction | Doesn't work for most toxins |
| Enzyme treatment | Phytates (phytase), lactose (lactase) | Commercial application |
| None effective | Tetrodotoxin, ricin, certain heavy metals, persistent organics | Ban list candidates |

---

## Detailed Method Profiles

### 1. Boiling
**Mechanism:** Water-soluble compounds leach from the food into the boiling water. Heat also denatures protein-based antinutrients (lectins, trypsin inhibitors). Discard water to remove extracted compounds.

**Effectiveness by compound:**
- Oxalic acid: 30–87% reduction (spinach, beet greens)
- Phytohaemagglutinin (kidney bean lectin): >99% at rolling boil for 10 min
- Phytic acid: 20–60% reduction
- Nitrates: 40–70% reduction (water-soluble)
- Amygdalin (HCN precursor): 60–80% reduction
- Glucosinolates: 30–60% reduction

**Critical rule:** Always discard the cooking water — it concentrates the extracted toxins.

---

### 2. Soaking
**Mechanism:** Passive diffusion of water-soluble compounds into soaking water over time. No heat required.

**Effectiveness:**
- Lectins: 40–50% reduction after 12h
- Phytic acid: 20–50% after 12–24h
- Tannins: 30–60% after 8–12h

**Requirements:** Cold or room-temperature water. Multiple water changes improve effectiveness.

---

### 3. Fermentation
**Mechanism:** Microbial enzymes (phytase, protease, lipase from bacteria and yeasts) actively break down antinutrients. Acidification further alters compound stability.

**Effectiveness:**
- Phytic acid: 50–95% reduction (sourdough, fermented porridge)
- Lactose: 20–40% reduction (yogurt), near-complete in aged cheese
- Lectins: partial reduction
- Nitrates: 20–30% reduction in fermented vegetables

**Examples:** Sourdough bread, tempeh, miso, kimchi, natto, yogurt, kefir.

---

### 4. Pressure Cooking
**Mechanism:** Higher temperatures (>100°C under pressure) rapidly denature heat-labile proteins and accelerate leaching of water-soluble compounds.

**Effectiveness for legumes:**
- Lectins: >99.9% at 15 psi for 30 min
- Phytic acid: 50–75% reduction
- Trypsin inhibitors: >90% reduction

**Critical note:** Pressure cooking does NOT make slow cookers safe for raw kidney beans — the temperature in a slow cooker never reaches 100°C. Always pre-boil kidney beans for 10 min before slow cooking.

---

### 5. Sprouting (Germination)
**Mechanism:** During germination, seeds activate endogenous phytases and proteases that break down stored antinutrients. This is a biological self-detoxification process.

**Effectiveness:**
- Phytic acid: 20–60% reduction (grains, legumes)
- Lectins: 30–50% reduction
- Trypsin inhibitors: 50–80% reduction
- Glucosinolates: mixed — some increase, some decrease

**Time required:** 2–5 days at 18–24°C. Rinse sprouts 2× daily.

---

### 6. Roasting/Baking
**Mechanism:** Dry heat denatures proteins and Maillard/pyrolysis reactions degrade some organic compounds. Some toxins volatilize.

**Effectiveness:**
- Lectins in grains: moderate reduction
- Amygdalin in almonds/seeds: 60–90% reduction at 170°C for 15+ min
- Aflatoxins: 10–25% partial reduction (NOT sufficient — prevention is primary control)
- Goitrogens (cruciferous vegetables): 30–50% reduction

**Warning:** Roasting at high temperatures generates NEW harmful compounds:
- Acrylamide (starchy foods >120°C)
- Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons / heterocyclic amines (meat, >180°C)
- Advanced glycation end products (AGEs)

---

### 7. Not Neutralizable — Ban List Triggers
The following compounds are NOT eliminated by any domestic cooking or preparation method:

| Compound | Found In | Why Not Neutralizable |
|----------|----------|-----------------------|
| Tetrodotoxin | Puffer fish, some octopus | Heat-stable, water-insoluble |
| Ricin | Castor beans | Stable protein — domestic heat unreliable |
| Hypoglycin A | Unripe ackee | Requires full physiological ripening |
| Caramboxin | Star fruit | No cooking method documented |
| Mercury (MeHg) | Large fish, shellfish | Heavy metal; bioaccumulation |
| Lead | Contaminated foods | Heavy metal; no food preparation removes it |
| Persistent Organics (PCBs, Dioxins) | Farmed fish, animal fat | Lipophilic; bound in tissue fat |

*All effectiveness percentages are sourced from peer-reviewed literature. See individual molecule entries for PubMed citations.*
